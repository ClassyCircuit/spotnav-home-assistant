# Morek execution ownership, authorization and safety audit

Status: **A1 / Stage 1 substage 1 audit, proposed design, not implemented or approved**. Source audit 2026-11-08. No live system was queried or changed. Git status was clean before this document was created. Paths below are relative to `custom_components/spotnav/` unless stated otherwise. Source behavior is not necessarily the behavior of the installed HA version; the handoff records deployment drift.

## Scope and observed baseline

The existing session's `owner` in `core/session.py` describes ownership of a *charge* (`plan`, `person`, etc.); it is not a persistent, exclusive choice of controller for a physical connector. `SessionRunner` (`execution/session_runner.py`) invokes the pure `core/ownership.py:decide`, manages feeds and outcomes, and does not itself send service calls. Its callers execute verdicts; safety regulation deliberately bypasses a verdict. An Auto pause is also not an exclusive ownership interlock: manual actions and certain safety actions remain possible.

The experimental `api/morek_schedule.py:async_schedule_action` manually sends `SetChargingProfile` for a checked physical station/connector, profile 42001, stack 1, with final persistent 0 A. Its Send requires a valid current proposal, matching preview and `until_resumed` Auto pause. `clear` is a separate admin-confirmed `ClearChargingProfile(id=42001)`; `fetch` and follow-up `GetCompositeSchedule` calls are reads. The profile operation uses its **own per-entry lock**, not the legacy execution and assignment locks: the pause check alone does not serialize all potential competing writers. Status/preview is read-only; Send, fetch and clear require HA admin. A timeout can mean applied, and a composite result is effective limits, not a profile inventory or measured charging. No autonomous schedule executor or transaction authorization is implemented.

The handoff reports a historically matching readback after earlier mismatches, an unplugged car at that time, and a remaining final 0 A limit; those are historical reports, not current live state or proof of charging. Independent Morek/CT household protection and new-transaction authorization were not established. No current physical profile inventory is known.

## Preserve observation, planning and safety

- Keep `planning/auto_controller.py` proposal/price recalculation, `planning/auto_settings.py` inputs and revision, price feeds, SOC reader, vehicle identification, charger/connector status, power/current/energy subscriptions, session recorder (`sessions/recorder.py`), site measurement/calculation, diagnostics, notifications and Morek preview/readback. Separate `async_apply` from calculation: retaining only a preview without update triggers is insufficient. Keep other connectors' normal execution and load control.
- Retain the safety assumptions behind site current caps, phase wiring, fuse and margin, measurement freshness, start reservations, overload lowering, below-minimum pause and unconfirmed-lowering safety stop (`site/site_capacity_controller.py`, `execution/controller.py:async_apply_regulated_current`). Site control is best effort, **not a protective device**. Disabling it is not evidence of equivalent protection; unknown independent protection means schedule-only daily readiness must be blocked. Metering and calculating without an actuator does not provide the same protection.
- Preserve connector/physical ID guard, OCPP 6 A floor for ordinary current control, existing current limits and unpriced/stale-plan refusal; do not equate a 16 A time limit with actual power. Existing target/need-met stops and the immediate minimum-SOC floor are behavioral dependencies, not physical fuse protection. A fixed-time schedule does not replace their feedback semantics; expose that limitation and decide the replacement before daily readiness.
- Maintain read-only evidence independently of control: `MeterValues`/HA sensors and recorder may exist but their availability, freshness and provenance for Morek are unverified. A schedule/readback must never become actual charging history.

## Transaction authorization: observed and required

**Observed:** OCPP current profiles limit availability/current; the manual Morek implementation does not authorize a new transaction. Earlier observations in the handoff concerned an already authorized transaction, not a fresh plug-in. Neither matching effective limits nor an Accepted upload proves a car is authorized or drawing power. Repository code does not establish the installed station's authorization configuration or the vehicle's behavior. No credential, RFID, charger or vehicle setting should be changed silently.

**Proposed authorization plan, decision required:** establish by read-only inspection of the installed OCPP integration and documented station settings whether a new plug-in is authorized locally/free-vend, by RFID, by another user-controlled mechanism, or requires a remote start. Document who authorizes, timing, online/offline and reconnect behavior, and what happens when 0 A is active at plug-in. A future user-directed physical check may distinguish authorization, transaction creation and measured flow; it requires specific approval for any command/configuration change. If automatic charging requires a legacy RemoteStart, schedule-only cannot honestly be called autonomous without a separately approved authorization mechanism; do not infer that the profile starts a transaction or create an implicit exception.

## Proposed ownership and transition contract

1. Add an explicit persisted per-connector `legacy` vs `morek_schedule` owner, independent of Auto pause and charge-session owner. Migration defaults existing entries to legacy/unarmed; a previously sent profile and `until_resumed` pause remain untouched. Identity must resolve uniquely before entry; unknown/malformed state must not silently restore legacy writes when schedule ownership may be active. Define first-install versus corrupted/unknown migration explicitly.
2. Deliberate admin entry: show identity, current/known profile and uncertainty, transaction authorization, safety readiness and implications. Serialize transition against profile operations, controller operation/assignment and in-flight calls; stop admitting new legacy commands, drain or mark unknown in-flight outcomes, invalidate timers/retries/owed writes, then persist the owner before arming. A failed/ambiguous transition remains interlocked and visible, not automatically switched to legacy. Do not issue a profile or stop just by selecting a setting.
3. While owned, only the explicitly authorized schedule path may set/replace the owned profile. Manual Send and any future worker must share serialization, identity and validation. Explicitly distinguish suspend uploads (stored schedule continues), pause charging (requires a separately designed profile action), remove owned profile (may increase current), and leaving mode. The existing Auto pause is not any of those. A2 must not introduce autonomous uploads.
4. Deliberate exit: keep legacy blocked while user reviews the possibly persistent 0 A/profile and possible effect of removal. If removal is chosen, require the existing explicit exact-ID confirmation, handle unknown outcomes as possibly applied, and do not claim composite readback proves absence. Only after an approved exit policy resolves uncertainty and protection/authorization consequences may legacy be enabled; no automatic clear, start, resume or old-plan replay. Entry deletion/failed persistence/restart must obey the same no-surprise rule. Exact operator acknowledgement and whether exit may retain a profile remain decisions, not implemented guarantees.

## Decisions and open questions

1. Which independent, correctly installed and configured household load protection actually governs this connector, including when HA is offline? Is any active SpotNav site regulator currently needed? Who approves the safety evidence and the behavior if it is absent or fails?
2. How does this particular charger authorize a **new** transaction, including plug-in during a 0 A window and offline/reconnect? Is user RFID intervention acceptable for daily operation? Is there an approved non-legacy authorization path?
3. What are the exact entry/exit and deletion semantics when profile 42001 may persist, removal may increase current, or an upload/clear times out? Who acknowledges uncertainty, and may legacy ever be enabled while the owned profile may still exist?
4. How should the user pause charging, charge now, suspend automatic uploads and resume, without ambiguous Auto semantics or a second writer? Which users may invoke each action?
5. What replaces the existing live target/energy stop and immediate minimum-SOC floor, and how is the loss of feedback communicated? What are the active-window, stale/no-price and schedule-expiry policies?
6. Are other HA automations, OCPP integrations/controllers or charger-side profiles active, and how can their ownership be identified without treating composite readback as inventory? Is station identity stable through aliases, entry removal and reconnect?
7. How will locks and durable transition state survive process death with an in-flight external command? Which unknown outcomes require operator intervention rather than automatic recovery?

## A2.1 implementation note (2026-11-08)

The source now has **metadata only**, not exclusive execution. `execution/execution_ownership.py` uses a separate HA storage document `spotnav_execution_ownership` (HA envelope version 1, payload `schema_version: 1`). `records` maps SpotNav charger entry IDs to `{station_id, connector_id, owner}`, where station ID is the resolved physical OCPP ID, not the configured alias; `owner` is `legacy` or the reserved `morek_schedule` value. There is no public or internal operation to select or arm the latter. It may be decoded from a document, but is not proof that this release enforces it. It must not be written by hand as an activation mechanism.

During charger setup, before executor setup/restore, first use saves `legacy` only when the existing Morek identity helper resolves a supported physical station and connector. Offline/unresolved identity is not guessed or saved; first-use legacy remains unpersisted until a later setup can resolve it. Auto settings, session state and the manual Morek profile record are neither migrated nor modified. The store reads the HA storage envelope directly so malformed JSON is not renamed and then confused with missing first-use data; saves use HA `Store` with atomic writes. Malformed or unsupported data and failed writes are not overwritten with a legacy record. Multiple entry records for one physical connector are reported as a conflict, including across aliases. Stale records can therefore require deliberate later resolution; this release does not delete or hand them over.

The existing `spotnav/morek_schedule` **status** response includes `ownership`: `schema_version` (the schema this code understands), `record_state` (`missing`, `valid`, `malformed`, `unsupported_version`, `save_failed`, `identity_unresolved`, `identity_mismatch`, or `conflict`), `recorded_owner` (legacy on first-use missing; otherwise null unless the record is valid and matches), stored `station_id`/`connector_id`, and `schedule_mode_available`, `armed`, `ready` (all always false). `blockers` names missing command isolation, unverified safety and unverified transaction authorization; these are conservative fixed status indicators, not a live safety/authorization assessment. A status request reads but does not create or alter an ownership record. Manual Send's existing pause/identity checks are unchanged.

**A2.2 integration point:** metadata initialization is deliberately not a command gate. Loading before executor setup alone does not prevent legacy writes, including on corrupt/unknown data, startup/reconnect, regulator and deletion/handback. Implement admission and transition serialization at the writer boundaries in the inventory above, with a fail-closed policy for ambiguous persisted state and a deliberate safety design; do not block or disable fuse-related regulation without an approved replacement. Complete the test matrix before calling the connector exclusively owned. Safety, authorization and transition questions above remain unresolved. A2 is not complete.

Local checks: 10 isolated ownership tests and 30 isolated Morek tests passed using stubbed package/runtime dependencies; these are **not** the full HA pytest suite. Full pytest cannot initialize on this Windows environment because HA's test dependencies import Unix-only `fcntl`. Ruff, Python compilation, frontend build and `check-dist` passed. No hardware or deployment was involved.

## A2.2a proposed admission matrix (foundation only)

This matrix describes the new internal service, **not** existing production writers. It does not authorize hardware operations or settle safety, transaction authorization or transitions.

| Input at admission | Legacy actuator | Owned-profile writer | Read-only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unique resolved physical connector; matching, valid `legacy` record and explicitly unarmed status | Allow | Refuse: schedule mode unarmed | Allow independently of write admission |
| Valid matching `morek_schedule` record | Refuse: not legacy owner | Refuse: schedule mode unarmed; record is not activation | Allow independently |
| Missing, malformed, unsupported, conflicting, mismatched, unresolved or unavailable identity/ownership | Refuse; never use a missing record as a bypass | Refuse | Allow independently; caller retains its own read permissions and identity checks |
| Captured connector generation no longer current | Refuse stale write | Refuse stale write | Not a write-admission concern |

Missing metadata is deliberately not permission for this foundation, even though A2.1 reports a first-use legacy default. No emergency/safety-write exception is defined: a refusal must not be wired to a safety actuator without the unresolved protection review. Manual Morek Send/clear and all production actuator paths remain unchanged in this slice.

### A2.2a implemented interface and boundaries

`execution/command_admission.py` is internal and is **not imported by production writers**. `command_admission(hass)` supplies one shared instance; `physical_connector(identity)` uses the resolved physical ID and connector, never the alias. Callers must supply a fresh, trustworthy resolver with the existing identity-status shape; the Morek resolver itself remains restricted to its supported station/connector. `async_execute(entry_id, kind, resolver, async_callback, expected_generation=...)` returns `AdmissionResult(allowed, reason, value)` with stable reason codes, or propagates callback exceptions/cancellation. It resolves before locking, resolves again inside the physical connector lock and again after the ownership-store read; state is evaluated under that lock immediately before the callback. Refusal never invokes the callback. A valid `legacy` record is only admitted when status explicitly says unarmed; profile writes are always refused by this foundation, even with a stored `morek_schedule` owner. This is not an assessment of protection, charger authorization or manual profile safety.

`read_only` does not query or require ownership write admission. A read with a resolved connector uses the same connector lock and rechecks identity; an unresolved read can run without the lock. Its caller must still check user permissions, target identity and whether the operation really is read-only. Do not classify an operation that performs a write as a read. `generation(connector)` captures a process-local token; `async_invalidate(connector)` increments it under the connector lock after prior operations finish. A future queued writer must pass its captured token for stale-work refusal. Tokens are not durable, do not cancel a command already in flight, and are not a transition implementation. A timeout or cancellation after dispatch can still mean the charger acted.

### Intended lock order and integration work

- Future legacy sink integration must retain the existing outer order (Auto boundary/other decision locks, then controller operation lock where already used, then assignment lock where applicable), and take the **connector admission lock last**, immediately around final physical dispatch. A Morek writer with its existing per-entry profile lock takes that lock **before** the same connector lock. Never call admission recursively for the same connector; a profile operation that already holds it must not re-enter it for readback. No code holding the connector lock may wait for an Auto/controller/assignment or profile lock, or for another connector lock. Do not serialize the unrelated charger by entry ID alone.
- A transition must first quiesce or invalidate producers without holding a connector lock while waiting for their outer locks. Its durable ownership change, generation invalidation and admission check need a single reviewed atomic connector-locked protocol. The current standalone invalidation hook does **not** make a separate ownership-store mutation atomic; A2.1 initialization and future ownership mutations do not yet participate in this connector lock. Until they do, this foundation alone cannot close transition/initialization races or guarantee startup fail-closed behavior. Do not hold a connector lock and call an API that reacquires it.
- Before integrating any writer, reconcile readback and the Morek lock lifecycle, duplicate entry initialization, lock ownership, reentrancy, pending commands, cancellation/unknown outcomes and status reporting. Do not silently gate site regulator safety stops or current lowering. Safety and authorization, transition entry/exit, and handling of unresolved identity remain decisions; none has been resolved by these hooks. External HA/OCPP actors remain outside this lock. Schedule mode remains unavailable; A2.2 and exclusive control are not complete.

Full HA pytest still fails to initialize on Windows because `fcntl` is unavailable. Ruff, compilation, frontend build and `check-dist` passed. No live commands, deployment or restart were performed.

## A2.2b call-chain review — implementation stopped for coordinator review

**No writer was gated in this review.** The requested start/stop integration is not complete. The following observed interactions make a dispatch-only change insufficient to meet the requested state and safety guarantees; implementing only that change would give a misleading appearance of isolation:

- `ChargingController._start_command_locked` may restore phases, clear a manual target-stop record, reserve site headroom, write `AssignedCurrent` or a current number, and set `_start_write_pending` **before** `adapter.async_start`. `_plug_in_start_send` and `_async_start_window` arm a start retry before calling it. A late refusal can therefore leave or have already sent effects, or leave a retry armed; `_last_switch_command` is also set before dispatch and is not restored by the existing `False` branch. Moving the whole routine under the connector lock is not a safe shortcut: it takes the assignment lock inside that routine, reversing the A2.2a lock order relative to stop and risking deadlock. Current-write isolation is explicitly outside this slice.
- `_stop_locked` is shared by manual, automatic and regulator calls. `_regulated_stop` reaches it via `_stop_request_locked(balancing=True, ...)`; its `urgent` value varies, and a person's stop is urgent too. A dedicated internal regulator classification could distinguish that route, but `balancing`/`urgent` alone must not be treated as general authorization. A failed battery probe also invokes `async_apply_regulated_current(0, must_lower=True)` and reaches this path. Current lowering and regulator stops remain ungated and unchanged.
- Several caller-visible effects precede the stop: `_target_stop_body` sets the target-stop record and records completion before `_automatic_stop_locked`; other need-met/window-end paths also record completion before `_stop_locked`. `_immediate_stop_body` intentionally stores the person's pause even if the physical stop fails. `_stop_locked` sets `_last_switch_command` and `_stop_sent_at` before dispatch; on ordinary false it restores the latter but not the former, while cancellation handling differs from ordinary exceptions. Treating an admission refusal as the existing generic unavailable result without revisiting these callers would not establish the requested truthful state/retry semantics.
- The current Morek identity resolver returns a supported physical identity only for the connected, allowed Morek connector. A2.1 initializes metadata only during entry setup when that identity resolves. If setup occurs offline, a later ordinary command would see `missing` and the foundation would refuse it indefinitely unless a new, synchronized late-initialization path is added. Conversely, permitting all missing/unresolved identities or unconditionally skipping an unresolved alias could bypass a record belonging to that same physical connector. A generic OCPP charger can also be temporarily unresolved; the rules for proving an entry is unrelated without disrupting it need an explicit design. A2.1's initialization currently does not participate in the physical connector lock and can create a second record for the same physical connector. Resolving first-use, duplicate entries and identity-change races needs coordinated store/admission work before a safe production gate.
- `execution/charger_handback.py:async_hand_back_charger` directly builds an adapter and can start or restore current on entry deletion; it is outside this requested slice and remains ungated. Deferred/current writes, phase writes, Morek profile operations, external actors, startup/transition enforcement and the safety boundary also remain ungated. There is no exclusive schedule ownership or autonomous scheduling.

**Proposed narrowly scoped prerequisite for coordinator approval:** design and test a connector-scoped, serialized late legacy-record initialization and identity classification contract, including disconnected Morek versus unrelated OCPP, duplicates and save failure, without touching any actuator. Then split ordinary command preparation/dispatch/outcome so refusal can unwind or avoid pre-dispatch state and retry/completion changes while preserving regulator behavior and existing lock order. Follow with controller-level tests on a supported Linux/Python 3.14 HA test environment before connecting either sink. Confirm whether an ordinary failed/refused target stop should retain target-reached evidence while withholding completion notifications, and how manual-stop pause intent should be represented on refusal. These are not decisions made by this review.

Checks for this review: the existing 27 isolated admission/ownership cases passed using stubbed package/runtime dependencies. An attempt to run `tests/test_core_drives.py`, `tests/test_core_failures.py` and `tests/test_command_admission.py` with the full HA test harness failed before collection on Windows because `fcntl` is unavailable. Those isolated cases do not test controller dispatch. No code, charger state, HA configuration or deployment was changed; no version/build change was needed for this documentation-only stop.

## A2.2b prerequisite — serialized initialization

This note supersedes the A2.1 description above that initialization only occurs at setup and can create a second record. The subsequent identity-classification hardening below supersedes the original three-way classification. The initialization results include `identity_non_target`, `identity_target_unsupported`, `identity_unresolved`, `identity_changed`, `ownership_identity_mismatch`, `ownership_conflict`, `ownership_malformed`, `ownership_unsupported_version`, `save_uncertain`, `initialized` and `already_initialized`.

Startup calls `CommandAdmission.async_ensure_initialized` before the executor/restore, and the charge-control entity's unavailable/unknown-to-available state transition schedules the same metadata-only operation. The listener is registered before the first attempt and removed on entry unload; no polling or status-triggered initialization is added. This is an entity availability signal, not a proven OCPP reconnect event: if the identity becomes resolvable without that state transition, another lifecycle attempt is needed. An entry with no OCPP connector target at setup has no listener and still needs its target established by the existing configuration/reload path; no identity is guessed. No live behavior has been verified.

The operation resolves identity, takes the admission service's physical connector lock, resolves again, then takes the ownership-store lock and checks again before saving. Existing entry/physical bindings are never rebound or downgraded; a second entry for one physical connector returns conflict and does not create a second record. Both entries' status is marked conflicting once both have attempted initialization in this process; those contender observations are **not persisted**, so after restart duplicate ambiguity is rediscovered only as entries resolve again. Existing globally conflicting, malformed or unsupported documents are not modified. Different connectors have different outer locks; their storage writes still share the store lock. There is no store-lock → connector-lock path, recursive connector locking, transition API or actuator admission integration.

HA `Store.async_save` can swallow write errors, so a successful return is not enough: the code rereads the durable envelope and verifies the complete proposed document before reporting `initialized`. An exception or failed verification reports `save_uncertain`/`save_failed`; a later attempt rereads before retrying, and a write that actually landed can be recognized without another save. A cancelled executor-backed write may still land after cancellation, so this process refuses subsequent initialization retries as uncertain rather than racing it. External disk changes, power loss and recovery across a process crash are not an atomic ownership-transition protocol. Status requests remain read-only, and schedule mode remains unavailable and unarmed.

No ordinary start/stop/current/profile writer has been gated. Safety and authorization, target-stop completion semantics and manual-stop pause intent remain unresolved; A2.2/exclusive ownership is incomplete. The next narrow slice is a reviewed ordinary-start preparation/admission/outcome design with controller-level tests, preserving lock order and accounting for pre-dispatch phase/current writes, reservations and retries; do not implement stop semantics as an incidental part of that slice.

Local verification: 37 isolated ownership/admission/lifecycle cases and 30 isolated Morek cases passed with stubbed package/runtime dependencies; the lifecycle test executes the extracted setup function and is not a full HA integration test. Full HA pytest failed before collection on Windows due to unavailable `fcntl`. Ruff, compilation, frontend build and `check-dist` passed. No live charger operation or deployment was performed.

## Identity classification hardening

The existing `_identity_status` resolver now includes the number of physical matches and the uniquely matched point's protocol version; ambiguous matches take precedence over a wrong-connector reason. Classification uses those facts plus the shared physical Morek station and connector constants, not `supported=False` or a reason string alone:

| Classification | Evidence and intended future admission meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `supported_morek` | Exactly one physical match is the target station/connector, protocol 1.6, and the resolver supports it. It may be considered for legacy metadata initialization; it is **not** proof of exclusive ownership or readiness. |
| `non_target` | Exactly one physical match is a different station or connector. It is positively unrelated to this target, but this slice adds **no** bypass or actuator policy. |
| `target_unsupported` | Exactly one physical match is the target, but protocol is unsupported/unknown or the resolver does not support it. Initialization refuses with `identity_target_unsupported`; never treat it as unrelated. |
| `unresolved` | Zero or multiple matches, absent physical identity, or invalid connector. Offline configured IDs/aliases are not proof; initialization refuses with `identity_unresolved`. |

Manual Send/clear identity and safety checks remain in place; status is still read-only and adds diagnostic match/version fields. This does not address recovery or exclusive ownership: the control-entity availability signal is not proven OCPP reconnect coverage, duplicate contenders remain in memory and require rediscovery after restart, and cancelled writes remain uncertain until process recovery. Target-stop completion and manual-stop pause semantics also remain unresolved.

Local checks: 37 isolated ownership/admission/lifecycle cases and 37 isolated Morek cases passed with stubbed package/runtime dependencies; the lifecycle test uses an extracted setup function. These are not full HA tests. Full HA pytest failed before collection on Windows because `fcntl` is unavailable. Ruff, compilation, frontend build, `check-dist` and `git diff --check` passed. No live command or deployment was performed.

## A2.2b ordinary-start extraction — partial, not admission

`ChargingController._start_requested_amps` now isolates the existing read-only selection of explicit, setting, plan or remembered manual amps. It is deliberately invoked at its original position, **after** phase preparation and a possible manual target-stop save, so this extraction does not yet establish a pre-write preparation phase. `_start_command_locked` remains the command sequence and outcome handler; fully separating them without changing the existing semantics requires further work. No admission service is called.

`StartAttemptEvidence`, kept only in memory as `_last_start_attempt`, conservatively records whether phase/current preparation was entered, whether a site reservation/release was requested, whether a retry was already armed, and whether adapter start was not attempted, returned not executed/executed, or has an unknown outcome. Entering phase/current preparation means it **may** have written, not that a write definitely occurred. Adapter exceptions or cancellation after invocation leave `start_dispatch=unknown`; a false adapter result does not erase evidence of possible earlier phase/current writes. These fields are diagnostic only, not a charger ACK, persisted history, a session decision or a safety gate.

The observed physical-write order remains: `_restore_three_phases_locked` can call `_switch_phases_locked`, which may first write current then set phases; a start can next write `AssignedCurrent` or the configured current number under `_assign_lock`; `adapter.async_start` then sends a switch/service/button/current command; some paths write current again after starting, or defer a session-current write. Other earlier mutations include `_start_held_back`, manual `_target_stop` and its save, `_requested_current_a`, site reservation and `_start_write_pending`; immediately before the adapter call the controller sets `_start_in_flight`, `_command_unfed`, `_start_sent_at`, `_last_switch_command` and `_start_cause`. The two immediate scheduled callers arm retries before invoking `_start_locked`. Evidence makes these boundaries visible but does not unwind them for a future admission refusal.

Future admission must precede the **first possible physical write** in phase restoration, not merely `adapter.async_start`. A single check followed by releasing the connector lock before subsequent writes is not enough to prevent a transition race; wrapping the whole start in that lock is also unsafe because phase/current preparation takes `_assign_lock`. A reviewed design must retain outer Auto/controller locks, take assignment lock where needed, then connector lock at each physical dispatch, with a coherent generation/transition protocol and no recursive connector lock. It must also decide how to represent partial preparation and unknown in-flight outcomes in `CommandOutcome`, pending/session state and bounded retries before integrating a gate.

Existing semantics are intentionally unchanged: the exception/cancellation cleanup still releases the reservation and clears `_start_sent_at` and `_start_write_pending`, and existing session feeds may report the start as not executed even though the adapter could have acted. `_last_switch_command` is not restored on a false/failed adapter call. These are outstanding correctness questions, **not** solved by the new evidence; physical charging remains unknown after an interrupted command. Stop/target-completion, manual-pause and regulator safety policies remain separate. A2.2 and exclusive ownership are incomplete.

Local checks: nine controller-level characterization cases passed against the real `ChargingController` using a minimal standalone Home Assistant fixture and mocked charger calls; this is an isolated run, not the full HA test environment. The full start-preparation, reservation, window-retry and controller pytest invocation failed before collection on Windows due to missing `fcntl`. WSL Ubuntu has Python 3.12 rather than the project's Python 3.14, and the existing Docker daemon was unavailable; no environment or dependency was installed. Ruff, Python compilation, frontend build, `check-dist` and `git diff --check` passed. No live command, deployment or restart was performed.
