# Morek execution ownership, authorization and safety audit

Status: **A1 / Stage 1 substage 1 audit, proposed design, not implemented or approved**. Source audit 2026-11-08. No live system was queried or changed. Git status was clean before this document was created. Paths below are relative to `custom_components/spotnav/` unless stated otherwise. Source behavior is not necessarily the behavior of the installed HA version; the handoff records deployment drift.

## Scope and observed baseline

The existing session's `owner` in `core/session.py` describes ownership of a *charge* (`plan`, `person`, etc.); it is not a persistent, exclusive choice of controller for a physical connector. `SessionRunner` (`execution/session_runner.py`) invokes the pure `core/ownership.py:decide`, manages feeds and outcomes, and does not itself send service calls. Its callers execute verdicts; safety regulation deliberately bypasses a verdict. An Auto pause is also not an exclusive ownership interlock: manual actions and certain safety actions remain possible.

The experimental `api/morek_schedule.py:async_schedule_action` manually sends `SetChargingProfile` for a checked physical station/connector, profile 42001, stack 1, with final persistent 0 A. Its Send requires a valid current proposal, matching preview and `until_resumed` Auto pause. `clear` is a separate admin-confirmed `ClearChargingProfile(id=42001)`; `fetch` and follow-up `GetCompositeSchedule` calls are reads. The profile operation uses its **own per-entry lock**, not the legacy execution and assignment locks: the pause check alone does not serialize all potential competing writers. Status/preview is read-only; Send, fetch and clear require HA admin. A timeout can mean applied, and a composite result is effective limits, not a profile inventory or measured charging. No autonomous schedule executor or transaction authorization is implemented.

The handoff reports a historically matching readback after earlier mismatches, an unplugged car at that time, and a remaining final 0 A limit; those are historical reports, not current live state or proof of charging. Independent Morek/CT household protection and new-transaction authorization were not established. No current physical profile inventory is known.

## Writer inventory and proposed gate locations

All routes below are potential routes conditional on configuration/state, not a claim that this particular installation uses every one. Gate by the **resolved physical station and connector**, including aliases and duplicate entries; do not globally disable other chargers. Enforce at the last common command boundary under the relevant locks, as well as refusing misleading upstream actions. Recheck after awaits and for already queued work; do not rely on UI visibility, plan pause or `SessionRunner` alone.

`docs/ocpp.md` notes that the station maximum is not written by SpotNav; OCPP session current is unavailable without a transaction. HA OCPP or another external controller can itself install profiles or issue commands. SpotNav's gate cannot enforce exclusivity over HA automations, other integrations, charger app/RFID or another OCPP actor; identify and handle those separately. Do not use generic `ocpp.clear_profile`: it may remove others' protection.

## Preserve observation, planning and safety

- Keep `planning/auto_controller.py` proposal/price recalculation, `planning/auto_settings.py` inputs and revision, price feeds, SOC reader, vehicle identification, charger/connector status, power/current/energy subscriptions, session recorder (`sessions/recorder.py`), site measurement/calculation, diagnostics, notifications and Morek preview/readback. Separate `async_apply` from calculation: retaining only a preview without update triggers is insufficient. Keep other connectors' normal execution and load control.
- Retain the safety assumptions behind site current caps, phase wiring, fuse and margin, measurement freshness, start reservations, overload lowering, below-minimum pause and unconfirmed-lowering safety stop (`site/site_capacity_controller.py`, `execution/controller.py:async_apply_regulated_current`). Site control is best effort, **not a protective device**. Disabling it is not evidence of equivalent protection; unknown independent protection means schedule-only daily readiness must be blocked. Metering and calculating without an actuator does not provide the same protection.
- Preserve connector/physical ID guard, OCPP 6 A floor for ordinary current control, existing current limits and unpriced/stale-plan refusal; do not equate a 16 A time limit with actual power. Existing target/need-met stops and the immediate minimum-SOC floor are behavioral dependencies, not physical fuse protection. A fixed-time schedule does not replace their feedback semantics; expose that limitation and decide the replacement before daily readiness.
- Maintain read-only evidence independently of control: `MeterValues`/HA sensors and recorder may exist but their availability, freshness and provenance for Morek are unverified. A schedule/readback must never become actual charging history.

## Transaction authorization: observed and required

**Observed:** OCPP current profiles limit availability/current; the manual Morek implementation does not authorize a new transaction. Earlier observations in the handoff concerned an already authorized transaction, not a fresh plug-in. Neither matching effective limits nor an Accepted upload proves a car is authorized or drawing power. Repository code does not establish the installed station's authorization configuration or the vehicle's behavior. No credential, RFID, charger or vehicle setting should be changed silently.

**Proposed authorization plan, decision required:** establish by read-only inspection of the installed OCPP integration and documented station settings whether a new plug-in is authorized locally/free-vend, by RFID, by another user-controlled mechanism, or requires a remote start. Document who authorizes, timing, online/offline and reconnect behavior, and what happens when 0 A is active at plug-in. A future user-directed physical check may distinguish authorization, transaction creation and measured flow; it requires specific approval for any command/configuration change. If automatic charging requires a legacy RemoteStart, schedule-only cannot honestly be called autonomous without a separately approved authorization mechanism; do not infer that the profile starts a transaction or create an implicit exception.

## Proposed ownership and transition contract

1. Add an explicit persisted per-connector `legacy` vs `morek_schedule` owner, independent of Auto pause and charge-session owner. Migration defaults existing entries to legacy/unarmed; a previously sent profile and `until_resumed` pause remain untouched. Identity must resolve uniquely before entry; unknown/malformed state must not silently restore legacy writes when schedule ownership may be active. Define first-install versus corrupted/unknown migration explicitly.
2. Deliberate admin entry: show identity, current/known profile and uncertainty, transaction authorization, safety readiness and implications. Serialize transition against profile operations, controller operation/assignment and in-flight calls; stop admitting new legacy commands, drain or mark unknown in-flight outcomes, invalidate timers/retries/owed writes, then persist the owner before arming. A failed/ambiguous transition remains interlocked and visible, not automatically switched to legacy. Do not issue a profile or stop just by selecting a setting.
3. While owned, only the explicitly authorized schedule path may set/replace the owned profile. Manual Send and any future worker must share serialization, identity and validation. Explicitly distinguish suspend uploads (stored schedule continues), pause charging (requires a separately designed profile action), remove owned profile (may increase current), and leaving mode. The existing Auto pause is not any of those. A2 must not introduce autonomous uploads.
4. Deliberate exit: keep legacy blocked while user reviews the possibly persistent 0 A/profile and possible effect of removal. If removal is chosen, require the existing explicit exact-ID confirmation, handle unknown outcomes as possibly applied, and do not claim composite readback proves absence. Only after an approved exit policy resolves uncertainty and protection/authorization consequences may legacy be enabled; no automatic clear, start, resume or old-plan replay. Entry deletion/failed persistence/restart must obey the same no-surprise rule. Exact operator acknowledgement and whether exit may retain a profile remain decisions, not implemented guarantees.

## Small implementation slices after review

1. **A2.1 — model only:** define versioned connector-scoped persisted ownership, safe migration and identity binding, explicit unarmed/transition/error states and load-before-restore ordering. Add status and tests; do not arm an incomplete mode or change charge behavior in isolation.
2. **A2.2 — isolation:** implement shared reason-aware, connector-scoped final write admission and transition serialization/invalidation, covering all inventory rows including removal handback, direct fallback, site regulator, deferred tasks and Morek's separate lock. Provide truthful refused outcomes and status. Gate upstream manual actions too. Keep schedule mode unavailable for daily use until safety and authorization requirements are resolved and all gates are tested.
3. Separately design profile-based pause/exit, independent safety integration and authorization with the coordinator/user. A3/A4, not this audit or A2.1, own autonomous reconciliation/recovery. Do not erase the present manual interlock until equivalent exclusivity is fully implemented and verified.

## Decisions and open questions

1. Which independent, correctly installed and configured household load protection actually governs this connector, including when HA is offline? Is any active SpotNav site regulator currently needed? Who approves the safety evidence and the behavior if it is absent or fails?
2. How does this particular charger authorize a **new** transaction, including plug-in during a 0 A window and offline/reconnect? Is user RFID intervention acceptable for daily operation? Is there an approved non-legacy authorization path?
3. What are the exact entry/exit and deletion semantics when profile 42001 may persist, removal may increase current, or an upload/clear times out? Who acknowledges uncertainty, and may legacy ever be enabled while the owned profile may still exist?
4. How should the user pause charging, charge now, suspend automatic uploads and resume, without ambiguous Auto semantics or a second writer? Which users may invoke each action?
5. What replaces the existing live target/energy stop and immediate minimum-SOC floor, and how is the loss of feedback communicated? What are the active-window, stale/no-price and schedule-expiry policies?
6. Are other HA automations, OCPP integrations/controllers or charger-side profiles active, and how can their ownership be identified without treating composite readback as inventory? Is station identity stable through aliases, entry removal and reconnect?
7. How will locks and durable transition state survive process death with an in-flight external command? Which unknown outcomes require operator intervention rather than automatic recovery?

**Recommended next task:** coordinator review of this audit and safety/authorization decisions, then A2.1's persisted, unarmed ownership model and migration tests; do not enable schedule-only operation until A2.2 isolation and the safety prerequisites are resolved.
